Epoxy Garage Floor Prep and Coating Notes: Adhesion Starts in Concrete

When a garage floor coating peels under hot tires six months after installation, the culprit is rarely the decorative color flakes—it is almost always skipped concrete profiling, trapped slab moisture, or oil contamination left inside concrete pores.

Coatings also involve reactive resins and curing agents. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) explains that volatile organic compounds (VOCs) from paints and coatings can build up rapidly in enclosed spaces without active cross-ventilation, and OSHA's solvent safety guidance emphasizes respiratory and skin protection during mixing and application.

Questions to ask before hiring a coating crew

Coating breakdowns, DIY trade-offs, and regional pricing notes are available on Epoxy Garage Floor Lab and its concrete floor coating guide.

  • How will the slab be profiled? Mechanical diamond grinding opens concrete pores consistently and removes old curing compounds far more reliably than a quick acid wash.
  • Do you measure slab moisture before coating? High moisture vapor emission lifts standard coatings unless a moisture-mitigating epoxy primer is applied first.
  • What is the exact solids percentage and coat count? Confirm the primer/base coat, flake broadcast coverage, and protective clear finish coat separately.
  • How are cracks and expansion joints handled? Rigid structural cracks need routing and flexible or semi-rigid crack-repair fill prior to coating.
Keep garage doors open with exhaust airflow directed outdoors during mixing and curing, keep pilot lights and water-heater flames isolated from solvent vapors, and keep children and pets away until full cure.
