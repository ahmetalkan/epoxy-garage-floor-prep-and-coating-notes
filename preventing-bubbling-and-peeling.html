Preventing Bubbles, Pinholes and Crack Telegraphing in Garage Coatings

Two visual defects frustrate homeowners right after curing: tiny craters or bubbles across the surface, and old floor cracks reappearing cleanly through the fresh coating.

  • Outgassing bubbles: Concrete is full of microscopic air pockets. If you apply resin early in the day while ambient temperature is climbing rapidly, air inside the slab expands upward through the wet coating and pops into pinholes. Coating as slab temperature is steady or gently falling—and using a thin penetrating primer—greatly reduces outgassing.
  • Crack telegraphing: Resin coatings are thin films; they cannot hold a moving concrete slab together. Active settling cracks will eventually split right through a rigid coating unless repaired with proper filler and monitored for underlying slab movement.
  • Traction additive: Glossy clear coats become slick when wet with rain or melting snow; broadcasting fine anti-slip aluminum oxide or polymer grit into the final seal coat reduces slip risk.
